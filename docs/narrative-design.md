# The Third Light — narrative design

The current build also includes a playable sea crossing, a four-station lodge investigation, a short signing recollection, an optional held-statement response to Mara’s draft in the archive, an optional four-source witness chronology at the detention gatehouse, an optional tower photo reckoning, a radio-house worktable confrontation, a four-station return to the north jetty, and a six-question reopened inquiry. Three short in-engine camera scenes mark the landing, Iris's rescue, and the daybreak jetty return; they can be skipped or played without camera motion. These deepen the opening and ending while animation, voice, and scene pacing remain condensed. A main-menu chapter picker supplies coherent earlier evidence in separate save slots.

This is the story specification for a **2–4 hour desktop investigation** on Greywake Island. The playable build currently has six short chapters, physical evidence, a Chapter 1 signing recollection, a separate Chapter 2 signing-room reconstruction and archive comparison, two player-aimed surveys, an intercepted-radio routing puzzle, an optional four-stop trace of Iris’s route, branching conversations, a radio-house confrontation, a staged rescue, a signed daybreak correction, a family handoff at the jetty, and the reopened inquiry. Longer scenes, performance animation, voice work, and pacing still need work before this is a full-length game. Every answer to the mystery has a physical or human cause. Weather, fatigue, reflections, and defensive memory make the player uneasy; they do not erase the evidence.

## The case in one paragraph

Seventeen years ago, the main rear navigation light became unstable in a gale. Keeper Elias Ward manually powered an eastern standby rear lamp while the lower front and western main rear lamps remained active. That made a second convincing light pair. From the seaward approach, the false line led west onto a reef instead of into the deep north inlet. Elias did not warn the approaching vessel and later altered the typed lamp export to say the standby lamp had been disabled. Junior analyst Mara Vale signed the finding that blamed the captain. She had noticed the missing physical relay record, but accepted the typed export and dismissed the captain’s account of a third light. Technician Iris Hale now finds the original punched relay strip, measures both sightlines, and asks Mara back to the island. Elias intercepts the radio channel and confines Iris behind a service gate; a storm turns his intended delay into a flood emergency. Mara must establish the true bearing, guide a rescue launch, free Iris, and correct the public record without pretending her own signature was harmless.

## People and their choices

**Mara Vale — player investigator.** Her professional instinct is to prefer a clean instrument printout over an untidy witness account. On the old case, she wrote “lamp status unresolved; obtain relay original,” then removed that qualification when the office was pressured to close the inquiry. She did not alter the log and did not cause the collision. Her responsibility is the certainty she lent a finding whose key input she had not verified. On the island, a false lamp in a window reflection briefly confirms what she wants to believe; the field survey shows why that explanation is insufficient. Her arc is from self-protective certainty to a precise, public correction. She should sound observant and capable, not suddenly irrational.

**Iris Hale — maintenance technician and independent investigator.** Iris finds the old relay strip while tracing the shared emergency power bus. She knows a single record could be called damaged or misread, so she prepares a reproducible pair of surveys, keeps copies apart, maps the pump and gate, and calls Mara because Mara signed the report. Iris is trapped because Elias locks the outer gate, not because she wanders helplessly into danger. Even from the tunnel she signals and helps Mara follow the right sequence. After rescue she decides what to publish, testifies to what she handled directly, and rejects anyone speaking for her.

**Elias Ward — keeper and island radio operator.** He first lit the standby lamp because he feared losing the only rear light in bad weather. He also feared that reporting the unsafe circuit would close the inlet and end his job and the island’s supply route. He made an immediate bad operational choice, failed to warn the vessel, and then deliberately concealed the choice in a typed export. Years of guilt do not excuse the cover-up or his confinement of Iris. The apparent “mainland control” voice is Elias transmitting locally. He uses calm procedural language to direct Mara away from dangerous evidence and only admits his location when the radio patch is found. A drafted but unsent rescue call shows another moment when he chose concealment over help.

**The captain and family.** The captain described a lower light and two possible upper lights. The inquiry treated that as stress confusion. A deckhand’s later addendum corroborates the second high light, but cannot replace the mechanical strip and sightline measurements. The family receives a corrected finding, not an easy restoration of seventeen lost years.

## Physical truth and fair mystery rules

The lower **front light** is at `(-100, -286)`; the safe **western main rear** is at `(-160, -171)`; the false **eastern standby rear** is at `(50, -211)`. West survey stake `(-186, -120)` lies on the inland extension of the safe pair. East survey stake `(232, -120)` lies on the false pair. Extended seaward, the false line crosses west toward the reef. The player must see both positions and identify the main rear plus front as the safe line. A rescue pilot refuses an unsupported or reef-side instruction; a mistaken menu choice never instantly kills them.

The case should rest on independent classes of evidence:

| Question | Evidence that answers it | What it does **not** prove alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Could a second rear lamp make a convincing pair? | Both field photographs, chart, tower circuit | Whether it actually ran on wreck night |
| Did both rear lamps run at 21:14? | Original mechanical lamp-command strip | Who powered the standby circuit |
| Who acted, and who changed the record? | Service order and later export stamp, compared with the strip | Why Mara accepted the export |
| Why was the captain dismissed? | Captain testimony, Mara’s draft and signed note, late deckhand addendum | That Mara altered the lamp log |
| Who is on the radio now? | Unplugged mainland patch and island-loop ledger | That every statement Elias makes is false |
| How can Iris and the launch be saved? | Pump bus tag, Iris’s bypass sketch, jetty soundings, true bearing | That a second lamp is safe to follow |

The rain-window “third light” is a real reflection with a mundane explanation. It is a useful warning about perception, but it cannot explain the captain’s sighting from open water. Do not reveal that the entire investigation, Iris, or the lights were hallucinated. The psychological tension comes from motivated interpretation of real observations.

These scenes give a main path of roughly **130–190 minutes**. Optional island records, environmental investigation, and rechecking the bearings can add **20–40 minutes**, yielding a 2–4 hour target without making the island physically larger merely to add walking time. Timings are design targets, not measurements from the present prototype.

The Chapter 1 recollection is Mara focusing on a specific past moment after she finds her working carbon. Players can inspect the warning she wrote, the pressure of the office closure bell, and the empty sleeve for the original relay column in any order. They then acknowledge that she accepted the typed export, crossed out her unresolved-status warning, and signed. A separate final choice states that this memory cannot establish whether the standby circuit was live. Wrong assertions return to the same fixed event; they cannot create an alternate history. Chapter 2 asks the player to check that memory against physical archive sources, rather than treating recollection as independent proof of lamp operation.

The optional gatehouse chronology tests how a witness account changes weight with its date. The captain’s wreck-morning intake statement predates publicity and matches the core of his hearing testimony. A deckhand’s second account supports the sighting but arrived a month after Mara signed and after the newspapers, so it cannot be treated as a document she ignored on signing day. The player must annotate Mara’s overconfident dismissal while leaving wreck-night switch state unresolved until the original mechanical strip is found. The physical board is mounted on an existing collidable registry plinth; progress saves independently and cannot be restored without the four inspected sources. This scene adds source reasoning, not a new eyewitness or a new historical record.

Mara’s optional archive response begins only after the signing-room reconstruction and the archive source comparison. She chooses whether to face her struck draft or the captain’s dismissed account first, then writes a present-day provisional statement owning both decisions. She keeps the old lamp state open, decides how to address the family without asking them for closure, and holds the statement in her journal or working folder. It is neither transmitted nor used as a new source about the wreck; the later signed correction must still rest on the relay strip and both surveys. An optional line at the family meeting can acknowledge that she prepared these words without implying the family received them.

The optional tower photo reckoning begins after Mara pins both fixed-stake photographs. It contrasts those reproducible bearings with the lodge's movable rain-window reflection, then has Mara annotate the old “stress confusion” margin in one of two honest ways while preserving the historical page. The last step leaves the 21:14 circuit state unresolved until the relay original is recovered. The scene uses existing observations, saves its ordered progress separately, and does not alter Chapter 3's required alignment or the later public correction.

The pump-house interior now gives the staged rescue a visible machine state: an original period diesel set and centrifugal pump with two collidable drive assemblies, valves and conduits, amber and green running lamps, gauges, a lowering tunnel water sight glass, puddles, and slow pipe drips. The center aisle and evidence remain reachable. These details respond to the existing backup-power and draining milestones; they add no new historical evidence about wreck-night lamp operation.

After Iris climbs out of the service hatch, Chapter 5 now requires a short safety-first conversation. Mara requests medical care, leaves Iris’s sealed originals in her hands, limits the first account to what Iris heard and felt at the gate, and lets the formal statement wait. The later Daybreak custody decision remains Iris’s choice. Character animation, voiced delivery, and longer scene timing still require further production work.

## Choices and consequences for the full version

- **When to transmit the evidence:** Mara may send an initial packet to genuine mainland control after finding the local patch, or wait until she has the mechanical strip and both photographs. Sending early brings backup sooner but lets Elias know his channel has been bypassed; waiting yields a stronger first statement but changes the rescue crew’s dialogue and arrival readiness. The player can still complete the rescue in either case.
- **How to confront Elias:** Present the service order and strip, ask for his account, or confront him publicly over the radio. His willingness to give a signed statement changes; his actions and Mara’s obligation do not. Empathy should reveal motive without becoming absolution.
- **Whether to disclose Mara’s draft:** At the hearing, she can volunteer the removed qualification or answer only when questioned. Both paths correct the accident finding, but Iris and the family respond differently to delayed candor. Hiding it cannot be a “perfect” ending.
- **How to handle Iris’s copies:** Ask Iris which originals she wants delivered to the inquiry and which copies go directly to the family. This is Iris’s choice; Mara cannot take possession by default. It changes the closing handoff, not the physical facts.
- **Safety before certainty:** The launch can always hold offshore while Mara checks a bearing. There is no arbitrary countdown that rewards reckless guesses. Weather raises urgency through sound, visibility, and Iris’s responses, while the pilot confirms the final instruction.

The playable build saves these choices explicitly and lets later dialogue reference only facts the player has found. Its chapter pacing is still condensed relative to the duration targets above.

## Production and verification gates

1. Implement the scene interactions and choice flags above without invalidating existing saves; version saves when the flags and chapter gates change.
2. Test each chapter as a fresh run and from a save; optional clues must not silently unlock required deductions. Test wrong bearing, safe bearing, delayed evidence packet, and each Elias conversation route.
3. Verify line geometry numerically and in browser photographs. The true pair must remain visible from the west stake; the false pair from the east stake; the seaward false line must still reach the western reef after any terrain edit.
4. Have a player unfamiliar with the solution complete the case and explain why each of the three central people acted as they did. If the player can only repeat the final answer but cannot identify the physical proof, the mystery has not earned its resolution.
